Cheap Print-and-Play (PnP) Netrunner

[Page last edited 05/02/2026]

You may have heard about Null Signal Games (NSG), the non-profit that rebooted Android Netrunner after Fantasy Flight Games (FFG) stopped printing new sets. Null Signal Games releases all of their card art free for people to print their own copies. This document contains helpful files and links for making your own sets!

Printing

The cheapest method I've found was to write some simple, hacky python scripts which extract the card images from the official print-and-play PDF's and constructs images with the right dimensions for printing as photos.

You can download the photos at these links:

  • System Gateway
  • Elevation
  • Vantage Point
  • Ashes Cycle
  • Borealis Cycle
  • Liberation Cycle

These photos are intended to be printed as 4x6 prints. Note that they are specifically designed for Snapfish, which auto-crops part of the image when they print it. After printing the cards come out to the standard 3.5x2.5" size.

Printing Options:

  • Snapfish -- This is the option I recommend, particularly when they have their unlimited "1-cent" (so-called) photo deals, which seem to run about once every month. They charge a lot on shipping, but you still get a good price per photo. In 2025 I was able to print the whole collection for under 7.7 cents per photo.
  • Walmart Photo -- Walmart has a consistent price of 14 cents per photo, yielding a total order price of $110.
  • [any photo deal you can find]

Sleeving and Storage

Photos are way too flimsy and see-through to use as cards without some kind of backing or card sleeve. Sleeving provides a nice looking card-back and some much-needed structural support.

Option A

Sleeve only your drafted decks in top-quality card sleeves: 100 matte "Dragon Shield" sleeves

Store your sleeved and your unsleeved cards in a box like this. You may wish to make some dividers of your own out of an old cardboard box.

Option B

Sleeve all the cards in some of the cheapest sleeves to be found: "Kitoyz" Red and Blue. My apologies that they are from Amazon.

Store them in a box like in option A.

For the flippable identities, you can either acquire some clear card sleeves or you can just tape two card sleeves back-to-back.

If you feel that these card sleeves are too flimsy or the backs are too see-through for play, you can buy a regular deck of cards and insert those into the sleeves of your drafted deck since a typical drafted deck is under 50 cards. Alternatively you could just have nice sleeves for your drafted decks, like in Option A.

Option C

Sleeve your drafted cards like in Option A and store them in a box like this.

Then store your unsleeved cards in two 2" D-ring binders with card sleeves for binders like these. Note that I have not tested these sleeve pages, so I cannot vouch for their durability. I also do not know if you'd need to sleeve the photos individually or if you can safely stuff the 3 identical photos per distinct netrunner card into a single sleeve.

Time You're Busy Cutting and Sleeving

When I get into a focused groove, I can knock out a set in about 1 hour. This would bring the total amount of work to 9 hours, but that's quite optimistic. Realistically you're probably looking at 14+ hours of work at least, especially if you get distracted reading the cards.
